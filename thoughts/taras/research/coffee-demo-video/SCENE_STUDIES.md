## Revised 27-second edit

| Time | Picture | Action and reason |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 s | Extreme bean detail in noir-rim light. | Move focus across the crease. Establish the material before revealing the machine. |
| 3 to 6 s | Low view along the conveyor. | Slide sideways past a foreground rail. Reveal the inspection bridge through parallax. |
| 6 to 9 s | Locked overhead view of the inspection area. | Let recorded beans cross the view. The stationary camera makes the inspection area easy to read. |
| 9 to 10 s | Tight blueprint view of the discharge assembly. | Orient the viewer to the nozzle and splitter before the slow-motion shot. |
| 10 to 15 s | Noir-rim slow-motion view of two beans. | Track a verified pulse that sends a defective bean below its good neighbour. Use recorded poses and contact evidence. |
| 15 to 24 s | Real continuous-engine UI capture. | Follow one injected object and hold its result card. Retain actual rolling scores and actual engine speed. |
| 24 to 27 s | High three-quarter machine view in warm clay. | Make a short lateral reveal, then hold the composition. Give the details their final spatial context. |

These durations are editorial proposals. The UI may need more time when we inspect the actual capture.
The original board shows the earlier 25-second timing. This table includes Taras's requested slow-motion shot.
Each scene remains a separate clip for later editing.

## Composition notes

- Give each shot a visible subject and a reason for its movement.
- Alternate detail, low perspective, overhead, schematic, UI, and complete machine views.
- Use cuts between changes of scale. Avoid repeated slow zooms and long dissolves.
- Place the full machine late in the edit. Keep the opening focused on the bean.
- Keep text and sound separate from footage so Taras can edit them later.

## What the mockup establishes

The board proposes composition, light, scale, and sequence.
