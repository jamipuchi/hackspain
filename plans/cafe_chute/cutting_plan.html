Cut list, sheet layout and assembly

Material: 2.5 mm foam board, two sheets of 50 × 100 cm; sheet 1 carries the structure, sheet 2 the base skirt and the tilt blocks. The two side walls of the chute run all the way down to a base tray, so the finished piece is one closed box girder. The slope is changed with a tilt block under one end of the tray, so friction can be tuned without touching the structure. All dimensions in cm; board thickness T = 0.25 is already included in every number.

Fixed geometry

SLOPE
15° built in. 12°, 18° or 21° with a tilt block under one end of the tray (table below). Every length "along the chute" is measured on the slanted top edge from the top end.
CHUTE
40.0 along the slant = 38.6 horizontal. Inner width 2.5, outer 3.0. Walls 3.0 above the paper, measured square to the floor.
HEIGHTS
Floor top at the exit end 9.0 above the base plate, 19.35 at the top end. Wall top 12.1 and 22.45.
ZONE
camera zone 9.0 to 15.0 along the chute.
DOOR
right wall, 30.0 to 36.0 along the chute, full wall height 3.0. Hinge at 36.0. Swings about 25° into the channel.
RIGHT
your right when you stand at the top end looking down the chute towards the exit.

Cut with a fresh blade in two or three light passes against a steel ruler. Cut the two panels first, from the same edge of the sheet, so their slanted edges are identical. Foam board crushes if you press the ruler hard: hold it, do not lean on it.

Times are from the model in the 3D simulation (zone centre at 12 cm to the door at 30 cm) and are approximate. Subtract the door swing to get the laptop's budget from photo to command: about 0.1 s once the firmware's fast-door channel is in, about 0.35 s with the stock ramp. With the stock ramp only 12° and 15° leave a usable budget.

  • Start at 15°. Drop ten beans. If any stops on the paper, go to 18°. If they skip or bounce at the door, go to 12°.
  • The whole tray tilts, so the door, riser and cup keep their geometry. Only the bowl under the exit is on the table; at 12° the exit rises 2.2, so it still clears an 8 cm bowl.
  • Block placement: push the block under the skirt until the skirt sits on the block's top plate along its full 18 cm. The other end of the skirt rests on the table.
  • Phone: re-aim it after a change; the zone moves about 1 cm at the top end.

Assembly order

PVA or hot glue: every structural jointdouble-sided tape: paper liner, servo earssingle-sided tape: clamp while glue dries, cover fillets
  1. Cut both panels P from the same edge of sheet 1 (Fig. 2). Lay them face to face and check the slanted edges match within 0.1. Check: slant = 40.0.
  2. Base tray. Glue the two long skirts KS edge-on under the base plate K along its long edges, then the two short skirts KE between them. Tape square, let dry. Check: the tray sits flat with no rock.
  3. Floor lines and rails. On the inside face of each panel draw the floor line (19.35 at the top end, 9.0 at the exit end) and the rail line 0.26 below it. Glue rail R with its top edge on the rail line. Weight flat until dry.
  4. Door. On the right panel measure 30.0 and 36.0 along the slanted edge, draw both cuts square to that edge down to the floor line, cut the floor-line side too. Keep the piece and trim 0.1 off its upstream edge.
  5. Bottom pivot. Push the toothpick square to the floor into the panel's cut edge below the floor line, exactly on the 36.0 line, leaving 1.0 out. Press the door's bottom corner onto it and test the swing, then take the door off.
  6. Girder. Stand both panels on the footprint lines on the tray (Fig. 3), 3.0 apart outside to outside. Glue B1 between them at the top end (base to wall top) and B2 at the exit end under the floor line. Tape the outsides square and let the glue set. Then run a continuous bead along both panel bottoms onto the base. Check: 2.5 between the inside faces at both ends and in the middle.
  7. Floor. Slide F in from the exit end onto the rails, butt it against B1, glue along both rails and B1. Run a fillet of glue along both inside corners and cover each with a strip of single-sided tape. Let it dry 30 min.
  8. Buttresses. Glue the four G triangles in the corners of Fig. 3 (base and panel outer face). Hold each for a minute.
  9. Paper liner. Two strips of double-sided tape along the floor, then the paper from the top end down, matte side up. No wrinkles.
  10. Bracket. Glue S1 inner flat to the right panel's outer face with its upstream edge on the hinge line, top 1.5 above the wall top. Glue S2 on its downstream end and S1 outer parallel 2.0 out. Glue S3 on top, overhanging 1.0 upstream and 1.3 inward. Glue the two keepers S4 on the platform underside 1.3 apart, centred on the hole. Check: hole centre over the wall centre line on the hinge line.
  11. Servo. Wire it to D9, 5V, GND and send S 90 90 75. Push it up through the hole from below, shaft down, ears between the keepers. Double-sided tape on the ears, one wrap of single-sided tape around the bracket box.
  12. Door on. Glue the four-layer tab S5 on the door's top edge at the hinge end, then the two cheeks S6 on both faces lapping door and tab. Seat the door on the toothpick, flush with the wall, and glue the horn arm onto the tab while the servo holds 90. Cure fully.
  13. Soft stop. Glue pad SP on the left wall's inside face from 29.8 to 30.8 along the chute, standing on the paper. The door's free end must land on the pad, never on the bare wall edge.
  14. Swing test. S 65 90 75: the free end should just touch the pad. Adjust the angle by 1 or 2 degrees until it does. Wrong direction: use 115. Back at 90 the door must be flush, no step a bean could catch on. Check: 20 cycles, no rub, no creep.
  15. Cup riser. Glue the four C walls into a tube, C5 on top, then glue the riser to the base and to the right panel under the door (Fig. 3). Place the cup. Check: rim 0.5 below the floor at the door.
  16. Zone marks, camera, bowl. Tick the wall tops at 9.0 and 15.0. Phone 20 cm above the middle of the zone, lens square to the paper. Bowl under the exit. Ten beans at 15°, then adjust the slope.

Dry time matters more than clamping force. PVA on foam board needs 30 minutes before you load a joint; hot glue is ready in a minute but leaves ridges, so keep it off the paper side and the door edges.

Why this holds

  • Closed box girder. Two full-height panels, a floor glued on rails, two bulkheads and a base plate form a closed section. Foam board flexes as a flat sheet but is very stiff as the face of a box.
  • Rigid tray. The base is a skirted tray, so it can rest on a tilt block at one end without bending.
  • Nothing rests on anything. No books, no loose boxes. The tilt block is captured under the skirt along 18 cm.
  • Rails, not butt joints. The floor is glued along two 39.7 cm faces, not a 0.25 cm edge.
  • Buttresses and riser triangulate the tall, narrow girder against tipping.
  • Servo in a glued box, not on a 0.25 wall top: the ears are trapped between two keepers and the platform, and the box is glued to the panel over 2.0 × 2.0 plus its end plate.
  • Door on two bearings: servo shaft above, toothpick below, horn on a T-tab that cannot peel.
  • Soft stop. The door lands on a foam pad, so a fast swing (the firmware's door channel is capped around 400 °/s) does not hammer the wall edge.
