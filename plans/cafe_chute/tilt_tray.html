Stop, look, tip

The simplest robust version. A short feed chute drops each bean onto a 6 × 6 cm tray hanging from the servo horn. The bean stops against the tray's back rim and is photographed standing still. Then the tray tilts 45° to the left (bowl, Sin defecto visible) or to the right (cup, Para revisar) and comes back level. One servo, no door, no hinge, no timing budget, no firmware change. If a bean sticks, the software tilts further. All dimensions in cm; board thickness 0.25 is included.

Why this is simpler and more robust than the swing gate

  • No timing. The bean is stationary when photographed and when the decision is made. A slow classifier, even a cloud call, cannot cause a misroute; it only slows the cycle.
  • No door. No cut-out, no hinge alignment, no toothpick, no soft stop, no fast-ramp firmware. The servo does a slow 45° move at the stock ramp.
  • Friction is a number. Stuck bean: send a steeper angle. The chute slope never needs to change; the chute only has to deliver the bean, not time it.
  • Sharper photos. No motion blur, so the classifier sees a clean bean every time.
  • Positive routing. Gravity pulls the bean off the tray on the chosen side; there is no path to the wrong container.
  • Fewer failure modes. Left: a sticky bean (retry steeper), a bean bouncing off the tray on arrival (rim + soft paper handle it), two beans on the tray (feed one every 2.5 s).
  • Servo protected. Flush-mounted through the tower wall on its own flange, body inside the box. The tray hangs below the axis, so its weight helps rather than fights.
  • Cost. Cycle time about 2 s per bean instead of continuous flow. For hand feeding that is the feed rate anyway.

Assembly order

  1. Base tray. Skirt strips KS edge-on under K. Tape square, dry. Sits flat, no rock.
  2. Tower. Cut the servo hole in one T2 (1.2 wide × 2.3 tall, centred, bottom edge 10.35 above the tower's bottom edge). Glue T1 + T2 into a box, T3 on top. Glue it to the base on its footprint (Fig. 3), front wall towards the chute, with the two G buttresses on its sides.
  3. Servo. Wire it to D9, 5V, GND; send S 90 90 75. Push the body through the hole from outside until the flange sits on the wall; glue the flange and tape across it. Shaft points at the chute. Cable out through the open tower bottom.
  4. Hanger. Glue HP on the back of H around its centre, then glue the cross horn onto HP with H hanging straight down from the axis (top edge 1.0 above the axis). Let it cure fully before loading it.
  5. Tray. Glue TS edge-on under TR along its centre line, then glue TR + TS to the front face of H with the tray top exactly 1.0 below the axis. Glue TL on the front edge. Paper on top, and a paper strip over the inside face of the rim and lip so the camera sees only white around the bean. Level at S 90; tilts symmetric at S 45 / S 135.
  6. Chute. Panels P with floor line and rails as before; B1 and B2 between them; F onto the rails; glue the panels to the base from 3.0 to 17.1 on the centre line so the exit is 0.5 before the tray lip and 1.5 above the tray. Two G buttresses.
  7. Containers and phone. Bowl under the left edge, cup under the right (Fig. 3). Phone 20 cm above the tray, lens down. Ten beans: each should stop on the tray, then leave on the commanded side.
