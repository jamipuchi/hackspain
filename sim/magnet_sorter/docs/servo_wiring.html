Arduino UNO R3 · one micro servo · USB only

Conveyor Servo Wiring

Three jumpers, three pins. A single micro servo runs straight off the Uno's USB power, so the separate 5 V supply from before is not needed for this setup.

Why it kept spinning: in your photo the orange wire is in pin ~10. The firmware holds pin 10 at the shoulder angle, so a continuous servo there never stops. The stop button talks to pin ~6. Move the orange wire and the problem goes away.

Which wire goes where

Servo leadJumperUno pinWhere on the board
orangeorange~6Top row, right group. Second pin after the gap, next to 7.
redred5VBottom row marked POWER. Fourth pin from the left.
brownblackGNDBottom row marked POWER. Right next to 5V. Either GND pin works.

Do this, in order

  1. Unplug the USB cable from the Uno.
    Never move wires while the board is powered.
  2. Pull the orange jumper out of pin ~10.
    It's the one going into the top header in your photo.
  3. Push the orange jumper into pin ~6.
    Top header, right-hand group. Count from the gap: 7, then ~6. The label is printed right below the pin.
  4. Push the red jumper into 5V.
    Bottom header labelled POWER. It's between 3.3V and GND.
  5. Push the black jumper into GND.
    Bottom header, immediately to the right of 5V.
  6. Check the 3-pin plug on the servo lead.
    Brown servo wire lines up with the black jumper, red with red, orange with orange. Yours already looks right.
  7. Plug the USB cable back in.
    Expected: the green ON light comes on and the servo does not move. The new firmware sends no signal to pin 6 until you press RUN.
  8. Start the button panel.
    In Terminal, paste these two lines. A page with RUN and STOP opens in your browser.
    cd ~/robotics/magnet_sorter
../.venv/bin/python conveyor_button.py
  9. Press RUN to move, STOP to stop.
    Space or Escape also stops. Ctrl-C in Terminal stops the servo and closes the panel.

If it spins without pressing RUN

With the orange wire on ~6 and the board just powered, the servo has no signal. If it still turns, the servo or its lead is faulty. Swap in another servo to confirm.

If RUN just twitches to one angle

Then this is a normal positional servo, not a continuous one. RUN sets an angle and holds it. That's fine for testing; the conveyor needs a continuous-rotation servo such as an FS90R or DS04-NFC.

Adding the bigger arm servos later

The MG946R and MG90S arm servos need the separate 5 V 5 A supply. Then the rule changes: supply on first, USB second, and supply +5 V never into the Uno's 5V pin. Ask before wiring that stage.

Why the board froze earlier

Servos were plugged into signal pins while their power was off. The Uno back-fed them through those pins and hung. With this USB-only setup that can't happen.
